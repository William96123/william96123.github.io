Discussion Session Week 2

William Duan

ECON 101, Fall 2020

Q1

Suppose Dontos derives the following enjoyment from drinking beer:

Number of Flagons Total Enjoyment (measured in copper stars) Marginal Benefit
1 20
2 36
3 48
4 56
5 60
6 60
7 56

What is his marginal utility per flagon of beer? If one flagon of beer costs 4 copper stars, how many flagons would Dontos drink? What if it only costs 0.5 copper stars (also known as a groat)?

Q2

Assuming Dontos marginal benefit from beer is a linear function of the quantity of beer consumed, derive his demand curve.

Q3

If a tavern called the Green Dragon can supply beer at the following cost schedule:

Number of Flagons Total Cost (measured in copper stars) Marginal Cost
10 1
20 3
30 6
40 10
50 15
60 21
70 28

What is his marginal cost per flagon of beer? If one flagon of beer is priced at 4 copper stars, how many flagons would the tavern keeper sell?

Q4

Assuming marginal cost of producing beer is a linear function of the quantity of beer produced, derive the supply curve of the Green Dragon.

Q5

Suppose there are 100 taverns in Flea Bottom all with the same marginal cost as the Green Dragon, and every night, 1000 people (with exactly the same drinking habit as Dontos) frequent these taverns. What is the market demand curve and market supply curve of beer? What is the equilibrium quantity of beer consumed and what is the price?

Q6

Suppose due to a massive rise in religious fervour, demand for beer falls, what happens to the price and quantity of beer in the city? What if, because winter is coming and wheat is in short supply, the cost of making beer increases. What happens to the price and quantity of the beer? Describe a scenario that would cause price to rise but quantity consumed to remain unchanged.
